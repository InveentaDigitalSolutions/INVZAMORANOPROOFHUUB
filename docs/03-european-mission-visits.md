# European Institutional Mission — Visit Report

*June 22 – July 2, 2026 · Germany and the Netherlands.*

The delegation visited 15+ institutions spanning agricultural machinery, dairy technology, soil systems, frontier research centers, protected agriculture, seed innovation, energy–land integration, and horticultural marketplaces. Each visit is summarized below. Annex I strategic-relevance notes are integrated where they sharpen the point.

---

## CLAAS KGaA mbH — Harsewinkel, Germany

**Purpose.** Establish a direct institutional relationship with CLAAS while supporting Zamorano's acquisition of a CLAAS JAGUAR 930 and exploring long-term technical collaboration.

**Key accomplishments.**
- Established a direct working relationship with **Joerg Wesseimann**, Global Product Team Manager – JAGUAR.
- Presented Zamorano's current investment in CLAAS equipment and its long-term mechanization strategy.
- Discussed operator training, maintenance training, and technical support for the incoming JAGUAR 930.
- Initiated discussions on broader institutional collaboration.
- CLAAS requested that Zamorano prepare a description of its future equipment, training, and educational needs to explore technical cooperation.

**Strategic relevance.** The visit transformed a machinery purchase into the beginning of a strategic institutional relationship, integrating mechanization, technical training, and agricultural education. CLAAS represents system-level thinking — machine–soil–crop interaction, throughput optimization, data capture, and service ecosystems — directly linked to scaling productive units into high-efficiency, teaching-integrated operations where mechanization becomes part of the learning architecture, not just a tool.

**Next steps.** Communication with Mr. Wesseimann continues; CLAAS has reviewed the JAGUAR 930 specifications, and Zamorano will prepare a summary of medium- and long-term equipment and training requirements.

## GEA Farm Technologies — Germany and the Netherlands

**Purpose.** Strengthen Zamorano's relationship with GEA during dairy modernization, understand its technological and manufacturing capabilities, and evaluate alternative milking technologies under commercial conditions.

**Key accomplishments.**
- Received an institutional presentation at GEA's divisional headquarters and manufacturing facilities in Germany from **Hao Shen** (International Sales Support Manager, Conventional Milking Systems).
- Observed the manufacturing of GEA's robotic milking systems.
- Met **Esteban Romnero**, GEA representative for Costa Rica, who had previously visited Zamorano and is preparing a quotation for the new milking equipment.
- Visited a commercial dairy farm in the Netherlands operating a GEA rotary carousel milking system and evaluated it in actual operation (advantages, limitations, labor requirements, infrastructure needs).

**Strategic relevance.** GEA is a global leader in advanced dairy infrastructure — milking, feeding, manure management, hygiene, cooling, and digital herd monitoring integrated into coherent operational platforms. The two visits provided complementary perspectives (how GEA designs/manufactures its systems, and how a principal milking system performs commercially), strengthening Zamorano's capacity to evaluate dairy investments from both technical and operational angles and to design a dairy system where learning, productivity, and profitability are structurally aligned.

**Next steps.** GEA continues to support Zamorano through the quotation process. Zamorano should continue the technical dialogue, compare the carousel system with alternatives, and determine whether it merits further feasibility analysis as part of a future phase rather than an immediate change to current procurement.

## Campus Klein-Altendorf — University of Bonn, Rheinbach, Germany

**Purpose.** Strengthen the relationship with **Professor Ralf Pude**, Scientific Director, and explore future scientific collaboration in field-based validation of perennial crops, renewable plant resources, and sustainable production systems.

**Key accomplishments.**
- Overview of Campus Klein-Altendorf as the University of Bonn's principal field research station.
- Visited experimental fields and research platforms for perennial crops, renewable biological resources, and sustainable production.
- Learned about Professor Pude's internationally recognized research on **Miscanthus** and other perennial biomass crops (carbon sequestration, renewable raw materials, sustainable land management, bioeconomy).
- Discussed evaluating selected plant materials, production systems, and scientific protocols under tropical environments through future collaborative field validation.

**Strategic relevance.** The campus demonstrates how a university extends scientific work into real field environments — a model for Zamorano's own field-based validation systems where teaching, experimentation, and production are integrated rather than separated. The complementary roles are explicit: Bonn generates long-term scientific knowledge under temperate conditions; Zamorano offers evaluation under the biological complexity of tropical agriculture, creating a continuous pathway from scientific discovery to practical application without duplicating either institution's strengths.

**Next steps.** Future discussions will identify plant materials, production systems, and research methodologies suitable for comparative evaluation under tropical conditions, with opportunities for faculty collaboration, student participation, and joint field validation.

**Purpose.** Examine how agricultural production, renewable energy generation, and land-use planning integrate within a single operational landscape, and evaluate alternative agrivoltaic designs.

**Key accomplishments.**

It reframes agrivoltaics as an integrated component of sustainable agricultural systems rather than solely an energy-generation technology.

**Next steps.** Knowledge acquired will support Zamorano's evaluation of renewable-energy alternatives within future campus infrastructure, informing system design and the technologies most appropriate for tropical conditions.

## Agri-PV Demonstration Facility — Bedburg, Germany

**Purpose.** Evaluate how different agrivoltaic configurations adapt to diverse production systems, including high-value horticulture, while maintaining productivity.

**Key accomplishments.**
- Visited one of Germany's leading Agri-PV demonstration facilities (developed in collaboration with Forschungszentrum Jülich and RWE).
- Examined three complementary configurations: vertical bifacial systems, single-axis tracking, and elevated pergola-type structures for horticultural production.
- Discussed how architectures influence crop management, machinery operation, light distribution, microclimate, productivity, and energy generation; evaluated elevated systems for specialty and protected crops.

**Strategic relevance.** The visit broadened agrivoltaics from an energy technology into a flexible agricultural production platform supporting multiple farming systems — particularly relevant where commercial crops, horticulture, protected agriculture, livestock, and education coexist in one environment. It reinforced that agrivoltaic development should begin with agricultural objectives, with energy, crop performance, efficiency, and sustainability as complementary components of a single system. The installation allows experimentation with crop rotations, light dynamics, and yield responses under photovoltaic structures — directly relevant to integrating energy infrastructure without compromising biological productivity.

**Next steps.** The facility provides technical and scientific reference points for evaluating agrivoltaic opportunities within future campus initiatives and comparative field validation under tropical conditions.

## Forschungszentrum Jülich — IBG-2 Plant Sciences & Jülich Supercomputing Centre, Germany

**Purpose.** Understand Jülich's integrated research model in plant sciences, bioeconomy, digital agriculture, and advanced phenotyping, and explore long-term scientific collaboration.

**Key accomplishments.**
- Field visit to the **Agri-PV research platform** (AgriFEe initiative) where berry production is evaluated beneath photovoltaic structures as part of a broader bioeconomy strategy.
- Scientific presentations from **Dr. Nikolai Jablonowski** on sustainable biomass, perennial cropping, and circular bioeconomy.
- Reviewed research by **Dr. Robert Koller** and collaborators on advanced plant phenotyping and digital technologies for plant responses under dynamic conditions.
- Visited the **Jülich Plant Phenotyping Center** — automated seed handling, high-throughput phenotyping, robotics, rhizotrons, imaging, and digital monitoring from seed to field-scale.
- Strategic meeting with **Prof. Ulrich Schurr** and **Prof. Uwe Rascher** (Director, IBG-2) on future collaboration through complementary scientific capabilities, joint research, and tropical field validation.

**Strategic relevance.** Jülich is far more than a collection of laboratories — its strength is integration across plant sciences, engineering, environmental sciences, remote sensing, AI, robotics, digital agriculture, and bioeconomy, building interconnected platforms that generate knowledge across the full continuum from fundamental research to field application. The scientific ecosystem extends through long-standing collaborations with universities, applied innovation centers, and private-sector partners. Within this framework, Zamorano is uniquely positioned to contribute complementary expertise through validation of integrated production systems under tropical conditions — knowledge currently underrepresented in global research. Programs led by Prof. Uwe Rascher and Dr. Onno Müller already span Jülich and Campus Klein-Altendorf.

The **Jülich Supercomputing Centre (JSC)** provides the computational backbone (JUWELS; JUPITER, Europe's first exascale supercomputer), enabling large-scale simulation in climate, energy, materials, and biological systems — expanding Zamorano's horizon beyond field practice into data-intensive science.

**Next steps.** Continued dialogue on agrivoltaics, plant phenotyping, digital agriculture, sustainable bioeconomy, protected agriculture, and integrated production — including joint research, faculty/student exchanges, and complementary tropical field validation. The visit established direct institutional dialogue with IBG-2's new leadership during an important period of organizational transition.
